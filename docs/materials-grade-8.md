## (a) Topic and subtopic structure

**How a chapter opener looks.** Each opener has a full-width colored band with the chapter title in
white bold serif and a "Vissza a kezdőlapra" link on the left. Below it is an illustration
(portraits, photos of buildings, charts) and a short motivational paragraph in the chapter color. The
lesson titles are listed on the right in black bold serif, without numbers, and the last item is
always "Teszt – …".

The lessons listed on the chapter opener are the app's **subtopics**. The lesson-opener pages list a
lesson's **internal sections**. These are not separate subtopics. They feed the subtopic's `scope`.

The band colors were sampled from the screenshots. They can serve as topic colors in the app.

## (b) Style of the sample pages

### Overall voice and layout (all sample pages)
- **Tone:** clear, precise textbook Hungarian.
  - Explanations use the inclusive "we": "Keressük meg…", "Felsoroljuk…", "Megmutatjuk…".
  - Tasks and prompts address the student informally (te): "Ellenőrizd…", "Elevenítsd fel…".
  - Sentences are short. Definitions are one sentence with the key term in **bold**.
- **Level of detail:**
  - Every new idea is a boxed one-line definition or a numbered theorem, followed right away by a
    tiny numeric example.
  - Cases are compared side by side.
  - There is little prose between boxes.
- **Typography:** body text is a classic serif (Minion-like). Section headings are bold serif.
  Chapter-opener paragraphs are in sans-serif in the chapter color.
- **Interactivity in the book:**
  - ▶ buttons (black circle with a play triangle) next to figures start animations.
  - A dark charcoal button with a hand-cursor icon ("Elevenítsd fel az egyenlet fogalmát!") links back
    to an earlier concept.
  - A circled "B" badge on theorem boxes is probably a link to the proof (bizonyítás).

  For the app: tap-to-reveal steps and "recall" links fit the students' habits.

### `12-40-14`: Merőleges vetítés a síkra (3D figures; most important for the figure library)
- **Layout:**
  - A bold section heading, then the definition in plain text.
  - The key rule sits in a **pale yellow highlight box** (`#FFF8BD`).
  - A large figure on the right with a centred caption below it.
  - Then three cases side by side, separated by thin dotted vertical lines. Each case has a figure, a
    caption sentence and an ▶ button.
- **Plane:**
  - Drawn as a **parallelogram with rounded corners**, slanting to the right.
  - Filled with a soft **radial orange-yellow gradient** (centre about `#FED58A`, edge about
    `#FFEDD0`) with a thin grey outline.
  - Named by a **Greek lowercase α**, upright (not italic), placed inside the plane near the
    lower-left corner.
- **Points:**
  - Small filled black dots.
  - Labels are **capital letters in italic sans-serif** (Arial/Helvetica italic look), placed just
    outside the point.
- **Projections:** marked with a **subscript 1**: A₁, B₁ (italic letter, small upright digit).
  **Not primes.** When two projections coincide the book writes **A₁ ≡ B₁**.
- **Lines and segments:**
  - Lines are **italic lowercase** (p, serif italic).
  - Segments are named by their endpoints with no overbar: AB, A₁B₁ (italic sans-serif inline too).
  - The intersection point of a line with the plane is P.
- **Highlighting:** the segment and its projection are drawn in **red** (about `#D22B25`). All other
  lines are thin black or dark grey.
- **Perpendiculars:** the projecting lines are thin vertical dark-grey lines. **The part below or
  behind the plane is dashed.** The part of the slanted line p behind the plane is dashed as well.
- **Right-angle marker:** a **small arc with a dot inside** at the foot of the perpendicular. Use this
  everywhere, not a small square.
- **Captions:** centred serif text under each figure. Figure names appear inline in italic sans
  ("Az *AB* szakasz merőleges vetülete az α síkra az *A₁B₁* szakasz.", quoted only to show the
  formatting).

### `12-41-34` and `12-41-55`: equations (algebra notation and example format)
- **Definition boxes:** full-width **lavender fill** (`#CECCE5`), no border, centred text, key term
  bold.
- **Side notes:** smaller lavender boxes on the right.
- **Rules and properties:** white boxes with a **navy outline**, sometimes with a bulleted list.
- **Example labels:** "**N. példa:**", white bold sans-serif on a **navy rectangle** (≈ `#242E7C`),
  followed by the text on the same line. Parts are labelled *a)*, *b)*, with an italic letter and a
  closing parenthesis.
- **Theorem labels:** "**1. tétel:**", bold, inside a navy-outlined box. Numbering runs through the
  lesson: 1.–4. tétel.
- **Solution label:** "**Megoldás:**", bold, on its own line after a worked-example statement.
- **Example types seen:**
  - classify (one or two unknowns);
  - decide whether two equations are equivalent and say why;
  - check a statement on given equations ("Ellenőrizd, érvényes-e…");
  - show that two equations are equivalent, followed by a step-by-step solution.
- **Math notation:**
  - **Variables:** italic serif x, y.
  - **Multiplication:** a **centred dot** "3 · (x + 2)". Coefficients are juxtaposed: 2x, 5y.
  - **Division:** a **colon** ":".
  - **Other symbols:** a true minus sign "−" and "≠"; stacked fractions (x = 4/3 shown as a built-up
    fraction).
  - **Sets:** braces {1}, {0}. Membership is written "a ∈ R", with R as a plain capital.
  - **Named equations:** E₁, E₂, E₃ (subscript digits).
  - **Generic expressions:** capitals A, B, C.
- **Equivalent-transformation strip** (under each theorem):
  - It is drawn on a light graph-paper strip in a **handwritten marker font**: "A = B / + C" → (a
    gradient arrow, olive to yellow) → "A + C = B + C".
  - **The operation is written after a slash "/"** and the applied part is in **red**. The four
    variants are + C, − C, · C (C ≠ 0) and : C (C ≠ 0).
  - **Use the same "/ −3" style in the app's step-by-step solutions** (e.g. "2x + 3 = 7 / −3"),
    with the operation colored.

### `12-42-37`: prism drawings (the only 3D-solid style reference)
Four unlabeled solids sit in a row above the section list: a right triangular prism, a cuboid, a
regular pentagonal prism and a regular hexagonal prism.

- **Projection:** an **oblique parallel projection** seen slightly from above.
  - Vertical edges stay vertical and keep their true length.
  - On the cuboid, the front face is a true rectangle and the receding (depth) edges go **up and to the
    right** at a steep angle (about 55–60°), shortened.
  - Regular polygon bases appear as **vertically squashed polygons**, roughly a third of their width in
    height. A circular base would therefore be a flat horizontal ellipse, so cylinders and cones
    should use the same squash.
- **Edges:** visible edges are **solid black**, medium weight. **Hidden edges are dashed** (short dark
  dashes) and show through the translucent faces.
- **Fills:**
  - **Flat colors** (no gradients, no lighting), **one hue per solid**.
  - The **top base has the strongest tint**, the **bottom base a medium tint** (seen through the
    lateral faces), and the **lateral faces a very pale tint**. The solid therefore looks like
    tinted glass.
  - Sampled values (top / lateral / bottom):

    | Solid | Top | Lateral | Bottom |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | green triangular prism | `#99B775` | `#DEE8D1` | `#C1D5A8` |
    | yellow cuboid | `#FFF482` | `#FFFBD8` | `#FFF7B2` |
    | salmon pentagonal prism | `#F49D79` | `#FCDFD0` | `#F9C1A8` |
    | peach hexagonal prism | `#FED689` | `#FFF1DB` | `#FFE5B7` |
- **Labels:** none on these overview drawings. On labeled figures, follow `12-40-14`: italic
  sans-serif letters, dimensions or key segments in red, and right-angle arc markers.

## Notation the app must use

**Observed in the screenshots:**

| What | How it is written |
|---|---|
| points | capital italic: A, B, P |
| projection of a point | subscript 1: A₁, B₁ (not A′) |
| coinciding points | A₁ ≡ B₁ |
| lines | italic lowercase: p |
| planes | Greek lowercase: α (β, γ…) |
| segments | AB (no overbar) |
| right angle | arc with a dot inside |
| unknowns | x, y (italic) |
| multiplication / division | centred dot · and colon : |
| decimal numbers | decimal comma (Hungarian/Serbian convention; no decimals were visible) |
| sets and the reals | {…}; R |
| equivalent transformation | "/ + C" after the equation, operation in red |

**Not visible in any screenshot.** There is no sample page on prisms, pyramids or round solids with
formulas or labeled dimensions, so the letters below are **inferred, not observed**.

*Best inference: the Serbian-origin set.* The official program states the formulas as
**P = 2B + M, V = B·H** (prism) and **P = B + M, V = B·H/3** (pyramid). This textbook is a
translation of a Serbian one that kept even its Cyrillic images, and it uses the Serbian subscript-1
projection labels. So its solid figures and formulas most likely use the Serbian letters:

| Quantity | Hungarian term | Letter (inferred) |
|---|---|---|
| radius | sugár | **r** (diameter 2r) |
| height of the solid | a test magassága | **H** |
| base edge(s) | alapél | **a** (b, c) |
| lateral face height | oldalmagasság | **h** (hₐ, h_b for a rectangular base) |
| lateral edge (pyramid) | oldalél | **s** |
| slant height of the cone | alkotó | **s** |
| base area | alaplap területe | **B** |
| lateral surface | palást | **M** |
| surface area | felszín | **P** |
| volume | térfogat | **V** |
| π | | written **after** the factors: r²π, 2rπH, 4r³π/3 |
| units | | a space before the unit, superscripts: 5 cm, 12 cm², 36π cm³, liter as l |

Cylinder formulas in these letters:
- B = r²π
- M = 2rπH
- P = 2B + M = 2rπ(r + H)
- V = B·H = r²πH

Results are usually left in π-form (for example 36π cm³). Use π ≈ 3,14 only when the task says so.

**Conflict to resolve.** `PROMPT.md` §4a gives "m for height" as an example of Hungarian notation.
That is the Hungary convention, where the height is m, the base area T, the lateral surface P and the
surface area A. It contradicts the Serbian-origin set above. Recommendations:
1. Keep all solid-notation letters in **one shared constant** (e.g. a `NOTATION` object used by the
   figure library and the formula helpers), so a single edit switches H↔m, B↔T, M↔P, P↔A.
2. Record the choice in `DECISIONS.md` and ask the teacher to confirm it. The book's own figures
   would settle it in seconds.
3. Never mix the two sets. In particular **P** means *felszín* in the Serbian set but *palást* in the
   Hungary set.
